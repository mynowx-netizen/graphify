## Per-video notes

**1. 11 Problems (A)**
- Chapters: identify the problem, iterate, find the levers, make big changes, flip the problem, solve it elsewhere, solve several at once, study player behaviour. Find the root cause first.
- Prey added a cheap new enemy late for variety. Gears of War rebalanced enemies after active reload favoured experts. Dying Light fixed durability complaints by lowering enemy health.
- One forum thread says the fixes skew toward positive reinforcement.
- Sources: https://glasp.co/youtube/p/how-game-designers-solved-these-11-problems, https://www.uohere.com/how-game-designers-solved-these-11-problems/, https://famiboards.com/threads/game-makers-toolkit-how-game-designers-solved-these-11-problems.4016/

**2. GWAC (C)** – The description says it is the creator's thoughts on gaming feeling less fun. Inferred: player-side advice.

**3. 100 Games (B)** – The games that most helped Brown learn design; learn by playing widely. Space Invaders was "arguably the first game with the sort of difficulty curve that we'd recognise today." The Substack transcript was not readable. https://gmtk.substack.com/p/the-100-games-that-taught-me-game

**4. Laughability (C)** – Title only. Inferred: a genre history of Roblox hits.

**5. Designed Themselves (A)**
- Gunpoint, NecroDancer, Ape Out and Into the Breach were not one flash of genius. A description repost names "fail fast" and "follow the fun."
- Ape Out began as a time-looping stealth game with push/grab. NecroDancer began as Spelunky-style quick decisions in a turn-based crawler. Gunpoint emerged once prototyping started.
- Sources: https://ytscribe.com/v/kMDe7_YwVKI, https://www.resetera.com/threads/game-makers-toolkit-the-games-that-designed-themselves.269778/

**6. Secrets of Game Feel and Juice (A)**
- Covers screen shake, hit pauses, strong feedback when enemies take damage, creative camera work, and exaggerated sound and visuals (Hotline Miami, Super Time Force, Random Heroes).
- Juice is "doubling down on whatever your game is about," and can take months or years to tune.
- Sources: https://briefread.pages.dev/en/summary/secrets-of-game-feel-and-juice, https://amara.org/v/C3BGA, https://medienwissenschaft.uni-bayreuth.de/wp-content/uploads/GxD-10-Game-Feel.pdf

**7. Think Like A Game Designer (A)**
- MDA: mechanics are rules, dynamics are how players respond, aesthetics are how they feel.
- A high ammo cap makes play reckless (Doom Eternal, Diablo III). Scarce ammo makes it cautious and fearful (Resident Evil).
- Players create unplanned dynamics; health packs dropped on death got exploited. Choose mechanics that fit your vision.
- Source: https://dandylyons.github.io/notes/Clippings/How-To-Think-Like-A-Game-Designer

**8. Button Fun (A)**
- "The three basic components of fun." The digest lists impact, reward and challenge, plus fantasy.
- Impact: holding the button makes a bubble you shoot away. Lasting rewards such as XP engage more. Too much challenge frustrates, too little bores. Fantasy lets players take on a role.
- Sources: https://recapio.com/digest/can-we-make-this-button-fun-to-press-by-jonas-tyroller, https://twitter.com/JonasTyroller/status/1250042444051288065

**9. Cursed Problems (A)**
- A cursed problem has no solution because two promises to players conflict: skill should decide fights, but free-for-all rewards politicking and kingmaking. Something must give.
- Barriers remove actions that break a promise. Gates put bad states behind a challenge. Carrots reward avoiding bad states. S'mores make bad states fun (how Smash Bros handles free-for-all politics).
- Sources: https://idbrii.com/notes/cursedproblems/, https://dandylyons.github.io/notes/Clippings/Cursed-Problems-in-Game-Design, https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2019/presentations/Jaffe_Alex_Cursed_Problems_In.pdf

**10. Non-Combat / Social Mechanics (B)**
- S1E25 explores non-combat play through Echo Bazaar (now Fallen London). The forum notes top board games (Puerto Rico, Diplomacy) are non-combat. Specific advice unverified (inferred: negotiation and standing can be the core loop). https://forums.penny-arcade.com/discussion/161846/patv-tuesday-august-2-2011-extra-credits-season-1-ep-25-non-combat-gaming

**11. Synergies (A)**
- Synergies make players feel powerful and smart, since they find combos themselves. A few interacting cards yield many strategies (Rosewater's "lenticular design").
- Random offers force players to adapt rather than pick from a list. Synergies need planning and are hard to do well.
- Sources: https://lilys.ai/notes/356290, https://amara.org/v/C3BET

**12. Hard Games (A)**
- Released during the Silksong difficulty debate. Difficulty makes overcoming a challenge more satisfying, supports the narrative or tone, and steers how people play. Letting players adjust difficulty is another tool. https://recapio.com/digest/what-s-the-point-of-hard-games-anyway-by-game-maker-s-toolkit, https://gmtk.substack.com/p/whats-the-point-of-hard-games-anyway

**13. Juice it or lose it (B, talk write-ups)**
- 2012 talk: a plain Breakout clone is juiced live without changing its rules.
- Tweened brick positions with easing; bricks scale on ball hits; squash, particles, trails, sound; the screen shakes on every hit. Derived projects add wobble, brick faces and music. Motto: "maximum output for minimum input."
- Sources: https://garden.bradwoods.io/notes/design/juice, https://roblog.co.uk/2024/03/juicy-games/, https://www.richtaur.com/GameDevTreasure/post/juice-it-or-lose-it/, https://github.com/jsheff/juicy-breakout

**14. Grow a Garden / blabverse (C)**
- The video's argument was not found. Secondary coverage of the game: "22 million" is a concurrent peak (22.3M on 23 Aug 2025 per one source). Plants grow while you are offline. Play is relaxing and easy to start. The owners credit retention and social interaction. Critics call it "boring" or "brain off," and TD Cowen alleged bot inflation.
- Sources: https://www.pcgamer.com/games/survival-crafting/i-played-the-roblox-hit-thats-pulled-in-10-billion-visits-and-while-im-not-sold-on-it-i-can-see-how-its-cultivated-a-mass-following, https://gamesbeat.com/janzen-madsen-interview/

**15. Exploration (A)**
- Geographic (new zones), mechanical (experimenting with builds and combos toward mastery), content (secrets, new items, rare Pokémon), narrative (piecing lore together from clues).
- Debates whether dialogue choices help narrative discovery; admits more kinds exist. https://lilys.ai/notes/668360, https://thetvdb.com/series/extra-credits/episodes/5251427

**16. Crash Course #16 (A)**
- Play satisfies different needs, such as competition or make-believe. Bartle's 1996 player types: players are most satisfied by games that match their type. Flow (Csikszentmihalyi) is deep, time-flies engagement. https://rosetta.to/u/crashcourse/psychology-of-gaming-crash-course-games-16

**17. My GameDev Pal (C)** – No content found. Inferred to overlap with #16.

**18. Field of View (C)** – The description says seven developers were interviewed about five principles. The principles themselves were not found.

**19. Sid Meier, GDC 2010 (B, talk write-ups)**
- Player psychology, not maths or history, drives design. The player is an "egomaniac," paranoid and deluded; perception is reality.
- Civilization Revolution: testers felt cheated losing at 3:1 and accepted a 2:1 loss but not 20:10, so the game quietly made 3:1 stronger. Accounts differ on numbers.
- Players credit rewards to their own cleverness and read bad luck as the game cheating.
- Sources: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/gdc-sid-meier-s-lessons-on-gamer-psychology, https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2010/03/12/sid-meier-gamers-arent-logical, https://www.tomshardware.com/reviews/gdc-2010-borderlands,2580-4.html

## Top techniques for this topic

**1. Juice every core action.** Stack fast feedback on the most-repeated action: pop, particles, sound, flash, shake, brief freeze, number pop. It should amplify what the game is about. Backed by 6 (A), 13 (B), 8 (A, "impact"); 20 inferred.
- Pop: tween a part's Size or a GUI's UIScale (EasingStyle Back) with TweenService, then back. Flash: a Highlight on the target, FillTransparency tweened to 1.
- Burst: a disabled ParticleEmitter on an Attachment, `:Emit(n)` at the hit point. Sound: SoundGroups plus slight random PlaybackSpeed.
- Damage numbers: a BillboardGui TextLabel, tween StudsOffset up and TextTransparency to 1, clean up with Debris.
- Shake: a LocalScript multiplies Camera.CFrame by a decaying random offset via RunService:BindToRenderStep (or the DevForum modules EZ Camera Shake v2 / GG Camera Shake). Zero it when `GuiService.ReducedMotionEnabled` is on, and offer a slider.
- Hit-stop: `AnimationTrack:AdjustSpeed(0)` for a few frames on both fighters (DevForum suggestion).
- The server validates hits and fires a RemoteEvent; clients render effects locally.

**2. Match challenge to skill; make difficulty a choice.** Backed by 12 (A), 8 (A), 16 (A, flow), 19 (B). Use AnalyticsService progression and funnel events to find where players die and quit. Add frequent checkpoints. Offer optional hard modes with BadgeService badges. Start easy: younger users care less about competition.

**3. Prototype fast and follow what players do.** Backed by 5 (A), 1 (A), 7 (A), 3 (B). Greybox with parts and the Toolbox, then Team Test. Run small-group or weekend public tests (Roblox-recommended). Watch live servers and chat, and A/B test with Experiments. Keep the behaviours players invent.

**4. Make other players the content.** Backed by 10 (B); 14 and 4 (C) inferred. Official docs support this most strongly.
- Party play: `SocialService:CanSendGameInviteAsync()` then `PromptGameInvite()`; a friend bonus (`Player:IsFriendsWith`); move groups with TeleportService.
- Trading: a server-authoritative window where both players confirm, with a countdown and logs; `IsPaidItemTradingAllowed` for paid random items.
- Emotes: `Humanoid:PlayEmote`, with custom emotes as unlocks.
- Showing off: keep players' avatars, make cosmetics visible (trails, auras, BillboardGui titles), and add leaderboards, gathering hubs and a UI-hiding creator cam.

**5. Mechanics → behaviour → feeling.** Backed by 7 (A), 1 (A). Name the target feeling first. Keep tunables in a ModuleScript, Attributes or Configs. Change one lever at a time, and try "solve it elsewhere."

**6. Design for perception, not maths.** Backed by 19 (B), 16 (A); 17 inferred. For earned random outcomes only: pity counters in DataStoreService, streak breakers, and victory screens that credit the player's choices.

**7. Lasting rewards, visible goals.** Backed by 8 (A); 14 inferred; onboarding docs agree. XP bar with low early thresholds, a level-up "moment of joy" (tween, particles, sound), BadgeService milestones, and the next goal always on screen.

**8. Synergies players discover.** Backed by 11 (A). Tag-based upgrades in ModuleScript data that interact (burn plus spread). Three random picks per round. A popup when a combo fires. Each piece simple alone.

**9. Hide four kinds of discovery.** Backed by 15 (A); Roblox "surprise and delight" agrees. Secret rooms with secret badges, undocumented interactions, a collection index with silhouettes, lore notes.

**10. Work around cursed problems.** Backed by 9 (A), 1 (A). In free-for-all fighters where players gang up: a barrier (spawn ForceField), a carrot (a bonus for beating the leader), s'mores (chaos events that make pile-ons funny).

## Conflicts and caveats
- **"Addictive" framing (4, 14).** The audience is young. Avoid fake near-misses, FOMO timers and pay walls.
- **Sid Meier vs. Roblox rules.** Hidden odds tweaks are acceptable only for earned outcomes. Paid random items need numerical odds summing to 100% before purchase, odds that update when boosts are active, a benefit on every outcome, and a check of `ArePaidRandomItemsRestricted`.
- **Over-juicing.** Juice should serve the game's identity (6). Shake can cause motion sickness, so respect Reduced Motion.
- **Hard vs. accessible.** Video 12 defends difficulty, while Roblox says young players prefer exploring and socialising. Make challenge optional.
- **Grow a Garden.** The figure is a concurrent peak, there were bot allegations, and the peak later halved. Don't copy "boring" without the social context.
- The criticism of video 1 comes from one forum thread. Six C-level videos are unverified.

## Official Roblox guidance
- Get to the fun within minutes, keep tutorials short, don't gate the fun, give short/mid/long-term goals, and include hidden secrets and "surprise and delight" moments. https://create.roblox.com/docs/get-started/strategies
- End onboarding on a "moment of joy" (rewards, animations, celebratory effects). Use low early XP thresholds, tuned with Experiments and Configs. https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/onboarding
- Roblox is a hangout: parties and trading make invites part of play, avatars matter, and private servers should stay affordable. Young users prefer exploring and socialising, and fun beats fidelity. https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/design-for-roblox
- Invites: `CanSendGameInviteAsync()` in a pcall, then `PromptGameInvite()`. https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/promotion/invite-prompts
- With `GuiService.ReducedMotionEnabled` on, fade UI instead of flying it in and stop looping motion. https://devforum.roblox.com/t/introducing-accessibility-settings/2723187
- Paid random items (May 2026): numerical odds, no "lose your payment" outcomes, and PolicyService checks for buying and trading. https://devforum.roblox.com/t/clarifying-requirements-for-paid-random-items/4654622
